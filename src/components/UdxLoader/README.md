# UdxLoader (reprise)

A dependency-free React loader on a clean white background (an optional paper texture is available). The sequence:

1. It types `UDX✝` in a thin monospace, with the orange cursor right after the cross. The type is sized with the logo (about 75 px on most screens), so the logo later replaces the word at the same size.
2. The cursor strikes RIGHT→LEFT through the cross, which shatters into tiny cross fragments on contact (they stay on the logo, never below or to the right of it). The cursor doesn't stop at the impact point: it keeps sweeping left at a steady speed and fades out (trail first) as it passes over the U. It is gone the instant the U is revealed, so it never travels further left than the U. Every cursor layer is then removed for good, long before the logo has fully emerged.
3. As it passes, it wipes `UDX` away and the supplied UDX logo is revealed RIGHT→LEFT in its wake. The logo slides in from the right and pushes out of the screen with perspective depth, then settles on a spring. Two small crosses drop into the U's channel and fade away.
4. Once every cross has gone, the logo glows once along its own silhouette: a restrained orange-red edge light (strongest on its lower contours) with a soft bloom that eases in, peaks briefly and eases out, leaving the logo plain black.

The whole sequence takes about 3.44 s. It is designed on a 3.96 s timeline and plays 1.15× faster (`TIMELINE.speed` in `timeline.ts`), so every motion keeps exactly its curve, easing and order, only quicker.

## Drop-in

```tsx
import { UdxLoader } from '@/components/UdxLoader';

<UdxLoader fullscreen onComplete={() => setLoading(false)} />
```

- **Vite, CRA and Next.js (app or pages router):** works as-is. It uses CSS Modules and starts with `'use client'`.
- **Not fullscreen:** it fills its parent, so give the parent a height.

## Timeline (deterministic, same on every mount)

Times below are design times. Real time = design time ÷ 1.15; for example, the strike at 1.26 s happens at about 1.10 s, and `onComplete` at 3.96 s fires at about 3.44 s. The public API (`duration`, `seek`, `getTime`, `onTimeUpdate`, `UDX_LOADER_DURATION`) uses real seconds.

| Time | Stage |
|---|---|
| 0.00–1.26 s | The cursor fades in and blinks. `U`, `D` and `X` land at 0.38, 0.60 and 0.82 s, and the orange `✝` lands right after X at 1.04 s. Each letter springs up in the cursor's wake, and the cursor ends immediately right of the cross. |
| 1.26 s → 1.38–1.58 s | **Strike and sweep.** One continuous RIGHT→LEFT motion: a 0.05 s smooth start, then constant speed, with no stop at the impact point. It is only visible until its exit (below), so it is never seen parked or travelling to the far left. The duration follows the distance (30 character widths/s, within 0.55–0.80 s). The cursor never moves right. The cross is sliced at the cursor's leading edge: each fragment breaks off at the moment the edge reaches its spot. |
| ~1.32–1.48 s → 1.38–1.58 s | **Cursor exit, keyed to the U.** The fade starts as the cursor begins passing over the U (the logo's U or the typed U, whichever comes first). It ends, at exactly 0, the instant the U is revealed: the cursor's centre is past the left edge of both the typed U (fully wiped) and the logo's U (fully uncovered). It lasts at least 0.1 s; if the cursor crosses the U faster, the fade starts earlier, but never before the cursor has cut through the cross. The ghost copies and the smear fade first. Then every cursor layer (cursor, both ghosts, smear and their container) is removed: opacity 0, `visibility: hidden`, `display: none`. It stays removed through the hold and after `onComplete`. Safety nets: from 2.0 s everything is removed no matter what, and once retired the cursor can't reappear for any later time, even after a resize or rotation. Only scrubbing or replaying to before the exit shows it again. |
| 1.27–2.30 s | **Seven pieces only below and right of the logo were removed:** of the 14 seeded pieces, the five flung downward into the open space under the logo's sloping bottom edge and the two small ones recoiling to the right of the X are left out, so no fragment ever appears below or to the right of the logo. The other seven keep exactly the same paths. The tiny cross-shaped fragments (exact miniatures of the glyph) are flung mostly upward, perpendicular to the cut; one drops behind the logo. They spread over a 30°–82° fan above "left" and carry part of the cursor's leftward push. **Around the logo:** the burst rises above the logo as in the original: every fragment stays within the logo's outer silhouette (the mark with its channels, notches, slits and counters closed and filled) or the band just above it (half the logo's height, only as wide as the logo), either where the logo comes to rest (the place the emerging logo grows into) or where it is on screen at that moment. As it breaks off it may also be at the struck cross itself, which stands just right of the X. Each piece follows its original seeded path, spin and timing; the moment its glyph would first leave that area is worked out once per layout (in idle time), and it fades out smoothly over 0.1 s just before, so nothing drifts off beside, below or far above the logo. They have varied sizes and smear blur, then slow to a drift. **Rotation is selective and follows each fragment's own path.** Only fragments launched on a clear diagonal (about 35°–55° from horizontal, 4 of the 7) rotate; the 3 flying straighter (flatter or steeper) keep the orientation they broke off with. Rotating fragments tumble in proportion to the distance they travel at an angle, so the spin is fastest in the burst, slows as they slow, and stops once they fall straight down. They also follow part of their path's turn as gravity bends it into a fall. Each has its own rate (smaller pieces spin a little faster). Upward- and downward-flung pieces spin in mirror directions, and none ever reverses. **Always upright:** no cross is ever upside down or even sideways. Up to 35° from upright, a fragment turns exactly as its motion demands. Beyond that, its turn eases off smoothly, like a piece settling as it slows, and it never passes 60°. The ease-off has no kink, stop, jump or reversal. The fragments that don't rotate, and every spin up to 35°, are exactly as before. **Visibility:** every piece is drawn 10 % larger, the small ones up to 35 % larger (the smallest the most), and no stroke is ever thinner than 1.5 px, so even the tiniest read as crosses rather than faint hairlines. This is drawing only: the count, paths, rotation, timing, colours and masking are unchanged. **Depth:** the fragments fly *behind* the logo, occluded by its actual ink shape. Their canvas sits beneath the logo layer, which is multiplied onto the background: black ink hides them pixel by pixel, so they disappear progressively as they slide behind a letter. Every negative space, including the D's counter, the slits between letters and the notches, stays see-through, so fragments keep moving visibly through it. **Sinking behind the lower part:** a fragment that sinks behind the logo's solid lower part (the D/X's lower band, the U's point) is clipped spatially at the logo's bottom edge. From the moment just before it would start to come out under that edge, everything of it below the edge is cut off, so it slides behind the edge as it falls and never appears underneath. There is no opacity change. The edge is read from the logo's own pixels (narrow notches such as the slits are bridged) and follows the logo's exact on-screen pose. This applies only once the logo is solid, and only to a falling fragment that was partly behind the ink. Fragments falling in the open space under the curve, or down a slit, are never clipped. No fragment is stopped or reset; hidden ones keep moving, they just aren't drawn. When each fragment reaches the edge is computed from its own path and the logo's exact on-screen pose. It runs once per layout, in small slices of idle time during the typing, so no frame is held up, and scrubbing back shows hidden fragments again. The logo itself is untouched and stays on top. If the logo's pixels can't be read (a cross-origin URL without CORS), the plain layering remains. |
| 1.26–1.50 s | **Reveal.** As the cursor passes, `UDX` is wiped away just behind it (soft edge). The logo shows from a little left of the cursor rightward, with a soft edge, so it is uncovered right→left in the cursor's wake. |
| 1.26 s → 2.0–2.3 s | The logo starts deep inside the screen at about 53 % size, grey and soft, 27 % of its width to the right and a little low. It slides LEFT into place, in the cursor's direction (critically damped, so it never moves back), while pushing out toward the viewer through a CSS perspective camera. Both motions are critically damped: a smooth start, a steady ease-out and no overshoot, so it never grows past its final size. Six depth layers behind its face form an extruded edge, then collapse into it. It has all but emerged (sharp, opaque, no depth layers) at about 2.0–2.3 s, well after every cursor layer has been removed, on every screen, and is completely settled before the glow begins. |
| 1.68–2.80 s | **Two crosses in the U.** After the strike, two small orange crosses drop into the U's inner channel from its open top, the deeper one first, so they never pass each other. Each starts gently and settles softly at its own height, about 58 % and 40 % of the way down the logo. They take 0.78 s and 0.68 s. Each has its own speed, a slight sideways sway that dies out as it settles, and a small tilt that follows the sway (at most about 11°, always upright). They stay inside the channel, behind the logo like the other fragments, so the U's black walls frame them. From 2.42 s and 2.50 s they ease out (over 0.38 s and 0.30 s), sinking a little further as they fade, well above the channel's floor. Both are gone by 2.80 s, before the glow. They are sized from the logo's own width, so they fit the channel on every screen. They appear with the supplied UDX logo only (its channel is measured). |
| 2.30–2.82 s | The remaining fragments fade out, each over its own short window, along their unchanged paths. All are gone before the glow begins. |
| 2.86–3.90 s | **One-time glow.** Only after every cross has gone (none overlaps it), the logo emits a single, restrained orange-red glow. It eases in (ease-in-out) over 0.42 s to its peak at 3.28 s, around when the logo comes to rest. It holds there briefly (0.14 s), then eases out over 0.48 s to nothing by 3.90 s. The logo is plain black again before `onComplete`, and the glow never repeats. **It follows the logo's actual silhouette:** it is traced from the logo's exact edges (read again at about its on-screen device resolution), so it hugs the angular corners, the U's point, the X's notches and the steps along the curved lower contour. It is traced from the logo's complete pixel mask and outlines the whole mark rather than pooling under it: every edge glows, the D's outer edges, its counter and its lower angled section included, strongest on those facing down (the lower contours), a little less on the sides and about half on top. **Edge light:** a crisp, emissive band on the black's own outer edges, hottest right at the edge and fading into the black within a few pixels. It is screened onto the ink only, so the logo's shape, proportions and sharp edges are unchanged and the black stays black. **Halo:** a tight glow just outside the edges. **Bloom:** a soft, faint aura shaped like the halo, hugging the silhouette (not a shadow below it). The narrow slits between letters stay clear (their walls glow), so the letters stay apart. Only the layers' opacity changes, so the glow stays locked to the silhouette. Scrubbing shows it at the scrubbed time. |
| 3.30 s | The logo is at rest: flat and centred on its ink, with no perspective, transform, mask or depth layers. On high-density screens, where the 236 px file is shown larger than its own pixels, a matching contrast filter keeps its edges as crisp as the original. It only steepens the grey anti-aliasing of the black-on-white mark, so the outline stays exactly where it is and the black stays black. This applies to the supplied logo only. |
| 3.96 s | `onComplete` fires: about 3.44 s in real time. |

## Props

| Prop | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `logoSrc` | the supplied logo (`udxLogo.ts`) | URL or bundler image object. Geometry and proportions are never altered. |
| `logoScale` | `1` | Logo size relative to the typed word. At `1` (the default), the logo's ink is as wide as the typed `UDX` at full size, and the struck cross and its fragments sit where they belong relative to the logo. The typed line and its cursor are drawn 12 % smaller than that (the cursor stays proportional); the logo, the strike sweep's timing, the logo's reveal and emergence, and the fragments' sizes and paths keep their full-size values. The type is sized so that the final logo is about 199 px wide on most screens (about half the width of a phone). It is kept proportionate and centred on its ink on every screen size, never wider than 62 % of the loader or half its height, and never larger than the file. |
| `maxLogoWidth` | the image's natural width | Upper bound in CSS px. The logo is never enlarged beyond the file. |
| `text` | `UDX✝` | Symbols at the END of the text are the ones the cursor strikes. Without one, the sweep and reveal still play. |

## Robustness

- **Placement:** the logo is centred on its ink. The typed line, cursor, struck cross and fragments are anchored on the logo's own centre, so their placement relative to the logo is the same on every screen. The strike sweep keeps its on-screen speed (measured in the original type size), so the reveal and the logo's emergence keep their timing.
- **Responsive:** the sweep distance (to the left edge of the loader) and duration, slice line, fragment spread, wipe and reveal edges, slide offset, logo size and depth are all computed from the live layout. On narrow containers the scatter is pulled in so nothing reaches the edge (tested down to 320 px wide).
- **Smooth rendering:** the cursor keeps the same size and is snapped to the device-pixel grid. The cross and fragments are drawn on a full-size canvas beneath the logo layer, and the logo layer (and its reveal mask) covers the whole loader, so nothing can be clipped. The cursor is removed as soon as the U is revealed, within the logo's own area. There are no React re-renders while it plays, and everything is cleaned up on unmount.
- **Glow:** built once per logo from its own pixels, in small slices of idle time during the typing, so no frame is held up. The outline's structure and the edge directions are worked out on the logo's own grid; the edge light and halo are drawn from exact distance maps at about the logo's device resolution (up to 2× the file), so they stay crisp on high-density screens. It is rebuilt in idle time if the logo is later shown much sharper (for example, after moving to a denser screen), as long as there is time before it lights up. If it isn't ready when it is due (a very slow device, or seeking straight into it), it keeps building in small slices per frame and eases in when ready, rather than holding up a frame. Per frame, only the opacity and transform of three prepared layers change. It is placed and scaled exactly like the logo, so it follows resizes. A missing logo, or one whose pixels can't be read (a cross-origin URL without CORS), has no glow, and everything else still plays.
- **Reduced motion:** a simple fade to the logo, with no glow.

## Font

The text uses "UDX Loader Mono", an 11.8 KB subset of the JetBrains Mono variable font embedded in `udxLoaderFont.ts`. It includes a `✝` glyph drawn on the font's own Regular metrics. The canvas cross and fragments use the same geometry (`CROSS_GLYPH` in `timeline.ts`). JetBrains Mono is licensed under the SIL OFL 1.1; the notice is in the file.
